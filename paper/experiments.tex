\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate every pretrained backbone on the same battery of tests, in the order below. The
first three measure action understanding on BABEL at increasing temporal resolution:
window classification over tiled windows, single-action classification under BABEL's own
protocol, and temporal segmentation. The fourth tests transfer to a clinical task, Parkinsonian gait severity on
CARE-PD. The last three put no task head on the backbone at all. They measure the geometry
of the features, retrieval by nearest neighbour, and how much of the raw signal the frozen
tokens still hold, through reconstruction and forecasting.

\subsection{Pretrained backbones}
\label{sec:exp-backbones}

\paragraph{Input representation.} Each frame gives every degree of freedom (DOF) five
channels: the joint angle encoded as $(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$, its first and second time
derivatives $(\dot\theta,\ddot\theta)$, and the generalized joint torque $\tau$ from inverse
dynamics. The body is the OpenSim model with its $43$ non-root DOFs; the six pelvis DOFs,
which carry global translation and heading, are excluded. Windows are $240$ frames
($4$\,s at $60$\,Hz), cut into patches of $8$ frames, so a window becomes a grid of
$30 \times 43 = 1290$ tokens.

\paragraph{The channel matrix.} We name nested channel sets by the channels they add:
\textsf{p} $=\{\sin\theta,\cos\theta\}$, \textsf{pv} adds $\dot\theta$, \textsf{pk} adds
$\ddot\theta$, and \textsf{pkd} adds $\tau$. A masked-reconstruction backbone is set by two
choices on this ladder: the channels the encoder \emph{reads} (input axis) and the channels
the loss \emph{scores} (loss axis). Crossing the two gives a $4\times4$ matrix of cells,
written (input, loss). A step along either axis only ever adds channels.

\paragraph{Objectives.} We pretrain three families with one encoder (a $6$-layer
transformer, $d=256$, $8$ heads) at a masking ratio of $0.9$ for $100$ epochs.
\textbf{MAE} reconstructs the masked tokens with a $3$-layer decoder. \textbf{SimMIM} has no
decoder: a linear head regresses the masked tokens, each standardized per token.
\textbf{JEPA} predicts the embedding an EMA teacher assigns to the masked tokens. JEPA has
no reconstruction target, so it has an input axis only and gives four cells.

\paragraph{Corpora.} The main corpus, \texttt{amass\_clean}, is AMASS converted to OpenSim
kinematics and dynamics. A control corpus, \texttt{amass\_smpl}, keeps the same takes in
SMPL's native parameterization: $21$ ball joints in a 6D rotation code, with no torque. It
therefore fills only the \textsf{p} and \textsf{pv} rungs of the ladder. On
\texttt{amass\_clean}, MAE and JEPA are pretrained with four seeds and SimMIM with three;
on \texttt{amass\_smpl}, with two or three.

\subsection{Evaluation principles}
\label{sec:exp-principles}

\paragraph{A convex probe.} Unless stated otherwise the backbone is frozen, its tokens are
mean-pooled, and a single linear layer is fit to convergence with L-BFGS under a per-label
binary cross-entropy. Each label is then a logistic regression over fixed features, so the
fit has one global optimum and no learning rate, warmup, epoch budget or seed that could
favour one backbone over another. The only hyperparameter is the weight decay, swept per
cell over $10^{-1}$ to $10^{-6}$ and selected on validation macro mAP. Repeating the fit on
one frozen backbone varies by $0.0008$ macro mAP, about a third of the spread between
pretraining seeds, so the probe does not limit any comparison below.

\paragraph{Floors.} Every task reports two references in its own units and on its own
split. \emph{Chance} is computed exactly from the annotations: for average precision it is
the class prevalence. The \emph{moments} baseline takes the mean, standard deviation,
minimum and maximum of every DOF and channel over the window. That gives
$43\times5\times4 = 860$ numbers, with no network and no pretraining, and they are fed to the
same linear head. It reads the same data, labels, normalization and metrics as the probe;
only its head is fit differently (AdamW, $40$ epochs, no weight-decay sweep), so if anything
this floor is slightly under-tuned. The moments show how much of a score was available
without learning a representation at all.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Cells are compared against the pooled seed standard deviation
$\sigma$ of their own matrix. Two tests guard against reading structure into noise. First,
we ask whether a matrix can be ranked at all: $n$ identical cells still spread, so the
observed range is compared with the expected range of $n$ Gaussian draws ($3.53\sigma$ for
16 cells, $2.97\sigma$ for 9, $2.06\sigma$ for 4). Second, cells trained with the same seed
share that seed's draw, so steps along an axis are tested \emph{paired} on the seed. They
are then Bonferroni-corrected over every step and total in the table.

\subsection{Window classification}
\label{sec:exp-babel-tiled}

\paragraph{Protocol.} BABEL-60 is multi-label classification over BABEL's $60$ most frequent
action categories. We tile $4$\,s windows over each validation take and label each window
with every category covering at least $15\%$ of it. That gives $4{,}936$ validation windows
carrying $2.52$ labels each on average. Training windows are random crops, extracted over
$20$ passes to recover augmentation. The primary metric is macro mAP, which weights every
class equally. Micro mAP agrees with it almost everywhere (Spearman $+0.91$) and is not
discussed further. Macro F1 is excluded: its fixed $0.5$ threshold makes it track the
selected weight decay (Spearman $-0.71$), while macro mAP does not ($-0.03$).

\paragraph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:exp-babel} gives the best cell of each objective, and
Table~\ref{tab:exp-ladder} the marginal level of each axis. The best MAE cell,
$(\textsf{pk},\textsf{pv})$, reaches $0.3734$ macro mAP against $0.3303$ for the moments:
$+0.043$, or $13\%$ relative. Chance is $0.0421$. Most of the distance from chance to the
probe is therefore covered by summary statistics, and every comparison between backbones
happens inside the remaining $13\%$.

Inside that margin, the MAE matrix is structured and not noise: its range is $10.2\sigma$
against a noise bar of $3.5\sigma$. The structure lies almost entirely on the loss axis.
Adding velocity to the reconstruction target (\textsf{p}$\to$\textsf{pv}) buys $+0.0229$
macro mAP (paired $p<0.001$). Adding acceleration on top of it \emph{costs} $0.0086$
($p<0.001$), and adding torque changes nothing measurable ($+0.0012$, $p=0.054$). Along the
input axis the whole ladder from \textsf{p} to \textsf{pkd} moves the score by $+0.0002$.
What the encoder is asked to reconstruct matters; what it is given to read barely does.

JEPA reaches $0.3433$ at its best cell, and unlike MAE it is best with the widest input
($+0.048$ from \textsf{p} to \textsf{pkd}). Its seed spread is six times MAE's, however, and
no single step survives correction.
SimMIM is far below both, and its worst cell, $(\textsf{pkd},\textsf{p})$, reaches $0.1508$.
This is not a failure of the probe. SimMIM's mean-pooled features are nearly rank-1
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-geometry}), and SimMIM is known to be designed for fine-tuning
rather than linear readout.

\subsection{Single-action classification (BABEL protocol)}
\label{sec:exp-babel-official}

\paragraph{Protocol.} The tiled windows of Section~\ref{sec:exp-babel-tiled} span several annotated segments. BABEL's own
benchmark instead cuts each chunk \emph{inside} a single annotated span and labels it with
that span's categories. We reproduce their procedure with three of its details:
\begin{itemize}
\item frame-level spans are used wherever the annotator produced any, and sequence-level
  spans otherwise, never both;
\item there is no minimum duration;
\item a span shorter than the chunk is looped to fill it.
\end{itemize}
The result is $8{,}152$ validation chunks carrying $1.37$ labels each. One deviation is
forced: their chunk is $5$\,s and ours is $4$\,s, because $300$ frames do not divide into
$8$-frame patches and the positional table is fit for $30$ time patches. We report macro mAP
alongside BABEL's own class-averaged Top-1 (Top-1-norm).

\paragraph{The loop gate.} Looping creates a shortcut. The loop period equals the span
duration, and duration depends on the action category, so a model can score by timing the
wrap rather than by recognizing the motion. We therefore rescore the $1{,}638$ chunks that
contain no loop. Their macro mAP ranks the MAE matrix the same way the full benchmark does
(Spearman $0.94$), so the ranking reflects motion. The absolute values are not comparable,
because long spans have a different class mix.

\paragraph{Results.} The pretrained backbone buys more over the moments here than on window
classification: $+0.065$ macro mAP, or $21\%$, against $13\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:exp-babel}). Single-action
classification is the less gameable of the two protocols. The top of the MAE matrix is a
four-way tie: the \textsf{pv}-loss cells at every input width score $0.3734$ to $0.3783$, a
span of $0.005$ against a per-cell seed spread of about $0.004$. Once again it is the loss
axis that moves the benchmark: it spans $0.025$ between its best and worst levels, while
the input axis spans $0.009$.

The protocol separates the objectives more sharply than window classification does. All $16$ MAE
cells beat the moments on macro mAP, one of four JEPA cells does, and \emph{none} of the
$16$ SimMIM cells does. BABEL's own metrics are much coarser instruments. Against the
moments, Top-1 puts the best MAE cell only $5.6\%$ ahead and the worst MAE cell $0.4\%$
ahead, whereas macro mAP separates the same two cells by $20.7\%$ and $8.7\%$. Our best
Top-1-norm, $0.281$, lies in the range of the published 2s-AGCN baseline ($0.24$). So does
the moments floor ($0.233$), which is the more telling of the two facts.

\subsection{Temporal segmentation}
\label{sec:exp-segmentation}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Window classification collapses time before the label is applied,
and summary statistics are order-invariant, so neither can express a target that changes
inside the window. We therefore resolve the target per time patch. Mean pooling runs over
the DOF axis only, which leaves a $30\times d$ sequence: one row per $133$\,ms patch and
thirty supervised rows per window. The same L-BFGS head is fit on those rows. Only
frame-level annotations are used, because sequence-level spans cover the whole take and are
constant across every window they touch. A patch is labelled by any category covering at
least half of it. The validation split holds $3{,}669$ windows, and the target changes at
$8.0\%$ of patch transitions.

\paragraph{Metrics and floors.} Per-patch macro mAP is the ranking metric, and weight decay
is selected on it alone. Boundary F1 scores only the transitions where the target changes;
it is reported swept over thresholds, because a calibrated head on a $60$-way vocabulary
keeps nearly every score below $0.5$. The moments baseline splits into two arms that
bracket the task. The \textsc{patch} arm computes moments over each patch's own $8$ frames,
so it has temporal resolution and no context. The \textsc{window} arm computes them over the
whole window and broadcasts one prediction to every patch, so it has context and no temporal
resolution. Boundary F1 has a second, binding floor: a predictor that claims a boundary at
every transition scores recall $1$, precision $0.080$ and $\text{F1}=0.148$, with nothing
fit at all.

\paragraph{What is recognized improves most.} On per-patch macro mAP the best MAE cell
reaches $0.308$ against $0.191$ for the \textsc{patch} moments: $+62\%$, where the same
comparison was $+21\%$ on single-action and $+13\%$ on window classification
(Table~\ref{tab:exp-babel}). The gain over arithmetic grows as the benchmark asks for more
temporal resolution, which is the pattern expected of a representation that encodes time.
The \textsc{window} arm reaches $0.184$, barely below the \textsc{patch} arm. Almost all of
what the moments score here comes from naming the action, not from locating it. The loss
axis again carries the matrix: \textsf{p}$\to$\textsf{pv} is worth $+0.025$ (paired
$p<0.001$), exactly as on the two classification benchmarks.

\paragraph{When it happens improves least.} Boundary localization barely separates
anything. The best MAE cell reaches a swept boundary F1 of $0.169$, against $0.157$ for the
\textsc{patch} moments and $0.148$ for the always-a-boundary predictor. SimMIM sits at
$0.150$--$0.153$ in every cell, indistinguishable from the constant predictor. The margin
of $0.021$ over that predictor is itself optimistic, because the swept metric picks the
best of $30$ thresholds on validation data and the constant predictor has none to pick. As a check that the
metric measures localization at all, the \textsc{window} arm scores exactly $0.000$: a
prediction constant in time is given no boundary credit. Whatever masked pretraining
teaches improves the account of \emph{which} actions a window contains far more than its
account of \emph{where} they begin and end.

\subsection{Clinical transfer: gait severity on CARE-PD}
\label{sec:exp-carepd}

\paragraph{Protocol.} CARE-PD~\cite{adeli2025carepd} pools gait recordings of Parkinson's
patients from four cohorts, each annotated with the UPDRS gait item ($0$--$3$). We follow
the paper's participant-level protocols exactly, and each is materialized as a named set of
splits:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{within-site leave-one-subject-out} (LOSO): one cohort, one patient held out,
  $110$ folds in total;
\item \emph{cross-dataset}: train on one cohort, test on another, $12$ ordered pairs;
\item \emph{leave-one-dataset-out} (LODO): train on three cohorts, test on the fourth.
\end{itemize}
A take is predicted by majority vote over its windows' argmaxes. The metric is the paper's
macro F1 over scores $0$--$3$, where score $3$ is dropped from the average only and never
from the data. Weight decay is selected once, on one BMCLab fold, and reused everywhere, as
in the paper.

Held-out predictions are \emph{pooled} over folds and scored once, never averaged per fold.
A LOSO fold holds out a single patient who often walks at a single severity, and this
distorts per-fold metrics in both directions. On T-SDU-PD, per-fold mAP reads $0.756$
against $0.385$ pooled, since AP is $1$ by construction when one class is present. Per-fold
macro F1 reads $0.157$ against $0.256$, since it divides by classes the patient never shows.
The protocol cell $(\textsf{pk},\textsf{pk})$ is fixed in advance rather than taken as the
best of nine, which would bias it upward. Two differences from the paper cannot be removed
by matching the protocol. Our data stay at $60$\,Hz, where theirs are $30$\,Hz. And our
inputs are OpenSim kinematics and torques, where theirs are SMPL meshes.

\paragraph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:exp-carepd} shows the fixed cell. Within-site LOSO has
no site shortcut, since it uses one capture rig, and there the probe clears the majority
floor on every cohort, by $1.7\times$ on 3DGait up to $4.2\times$ on PD-GaM. On PD-GaM,
the largest cohort, it exceeds the mean of the seven purpose-built encoders ($0.668$ against
$0.620$). On 3DGait it matches that mean. It falls short on BMCLab ($-1.1$ encoder sd) and
on T-SDU-PD ($-2.6$), which is the one cohort outside the published band.

Cohort size decides between training within a site and borrowing others. For PD-GaM
($1{,}700$ takes) and BMCLab ($781$), within-site LOSO wins, by $0.30$ and $0.11$. For
T-SDU-PD ($381$) and 3DGait ($90$), training on the other three cohorts (LODO) beats
training on the cohort's own patients. The crossover is monotone in cohort size. Averaged
by source cohort in the cross-dataset protocol, PD-GaM transfers \emph{worst} ($0.188$,
against $0.279$--$0.292$ for the other sources), which inverts the paper's finding. PD-GaM
is also the only cohort captured at $25$\,Hz and hence the most heavily resampled by our
$60$\,Hz pipeline, but we have not tested this explanation.

\paragraph{Neither the matrix nor the objective separates on this task.} For MAE only $1$
of the $12$ cohort-by-protocol blocks has a cell range above the noise bar, so the nine
cells cannot be ranked on CARE-PD. Across objectives, pooled over protocols, JEPA, SimMIM
and a thin-decoder MAE are within $0.01$ macro F1 of the standard MAE, and only the thin
decoder's $+0.007$ reaches $p<0.05$. That SimMIM, whose features are nearly rank-1 and which
fails the moments floor on BABEL, is level here shows how little a $4$-class severity
readout asks of the representation. One effect does replicate. Adding torque to the
\emph{input} (\textsf{pk}$\to$\textsf{pkd}) hurts cross-dataset transfer for MAE
($-0.045$), SimMIM ($-0.037$) and the thin-decoder MAE ($-0.041$), each significant after
correction.

\subsection{Representation geometry}
\label{sec:exp-geometry}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Before any head is fit, we measure the pooled features the convex
probe reads, through the probe's own pooler and normalization, on the $4{,}936$ tiled
validation windows. The \emph{participation ratio},
\begin{equation}
\text{PR} = \frac{\left(\sum_i \lambda_i\right)^2}{\sum_i \lambda_i^2},
\label{eq:pr}
\end{equation}
over the eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ of the feature covariance, counts the directions the
features effectively use. It is $d$ for an isotropic covariance and $1$ for a rank-1 one.
\emph{Linear CKA} compares cells pairwise. It is read against the CKA between seeds of one
cell, which serves as its noise floor. Tiled windows are deterministic, so row $i$ is the
same frames in every cell, as CKA requires.

\paragraph{Results.} No MAE cell is collapsed: all sixteen use $15.6$ to $29.1$ of $256$
directions. By contrast, SimMIM's features are nearly rank-1 on \texttt{amass\_clean}
(effective rank $1.1$--$2.5$), and on \texttt{amass\_smpl} its participation ratio is
$8.6$. This explains its probe scores before they are measured. The largest effect in the
MAE matrix lies along the loss axis. Scoring torque in the loss raises the participation
ratio from $16.6$ to $23.7$ on average (Table~\ref{tab:exp-ladder}), and to $29.1$ at
$(\textsf{pk},\textsf{pkd})$. The expansion vanishes once torque is also an input:
$(\textsf{pkd},\textsf{pkd})$ sits at $15.6$, a gap of $9\sigma$ against the pooled seed sd
of $1.45$. So the torque target is not inert. It widens the representation by nearly half,
in directions that a window-level linear action classifier barely uses: the same pair of
cells differs by $0.005$ macro mAP. The participation ratio tracks probe mAP only loosely
over the sixteen cells (Spearman $+0.70$, Pearson $+0.45$), so it measures something the
benchmark does not. Cells are further apart than seeds of one cell (CKA
$0.864\pm0.050$ against $0.936\pm0.022$), so the matrix axes do change the representation.

\subsection{Label efficiency}
\label{sec:exp-label-efficiency}

\paragraph{Protocol.} A representation is supposed to pay for itself in labels, which a
score at $100\%$ of the training set cannot express. We refit the convex probe on $5$, $10$,
$25$, $50$ and $100\%$ of the labelled takes and score each fit on the full validation
split, with the moments run through the identical head and fractions as the floor. We
subsample by take, never by window: windows are overlapping crops of a take, so $5\%$ of
windows would still touch nearly every take. The fractions are nested prefixes of one
shuffle. Weight decay is re-swept at every fraction, because the penalty is fixed while the
loss is averaged over rows, so an optimum found at $100\%$ over-regularizes at $5\%$. Every
fraction is scored over one fixed label vocabulary. Otherwise a small fraction, which loses
exactly the rare labels, would report a macro average over a different and easier set of
classes.

\subsection{Nearest-neighbour retrieval}
\label{sec:exp-knn}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Dropping the head altogether, each validation window takes the labels
of its $k$ nearest training windows under cosine similarity, weighted by a softmax with
temperature $0.07$, for $k\in\{1,5,\dots,500\}$. This asks whether windows of the same
action lie close together under \emph{any} weighting of the features, which a linear head
cannot show.

\paragraph{Results.} Retrieval recovers nearly all of the probe's score. The best MAE cell,
$(\textsf{pk},\textsf{pv})$, reaches $0.3645$ macro mAP against $0.3734$ with a linear head.
The four \textsf{pv}-loss cells are again the top four. The moments floor reaches $0.3315$,
equal to its linear-head score, so a linear head adds nothing to the moments and little to
the pretrained features. The pretrained gap over the moments is therefore a property of the
features and not of the readout. Every MAE curve is still rising at $k=500$, the edge of
the grid, while the moments peak at $k=100$, so the MAE figures are lower bounds.

\subsection{Reconstruction and forecasting}
\label{sec:exp-reconstruction}

\paragraph{Protocol.} The last test asks how much of the raw signal survives in the frozen
tokens. A small decoder (one layer, width $64$, about $0.4\times$ the encoder's FLOPs) is
trained from scratch on top of each frozen backbone. It is identical in every cell, and it
is kept small deliberately: the pretraining decoder runs at $8.6\times$ the encoder's FLOPs,
enough to compute torque from kinematics by itself, so a decoder that size would rank
decoders rather than backbones. The target is $(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$ in every cell,
since pose is the only channel every cell reads. The score is the mean per-DOF angular
error in degrees, taken through $\operatorname{atan2}$ on the held-out tokens. Two masks
share this rig:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Reconstruction} holds out $75\%$ of tokens uniformly at random. This is off
  pretraining's $90\%$ on purpose, so the number is not each cell's own pretraining
  objective under a new name.
\item \emph{Forecasting} holds out the last $8$ patches ($64$ frames, $1.07$\,s) across all
  DOFs, and publishes two model-free floors in the same units: holding the last observed
  frame (zero velocity) and extrapolating its velocity (constant velocity).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:exp-reconstruction} shows the full matrix. On
forecasting every cell beats both floors. The best, $(\textsf{pk},\textsf{pv})$, is
$4.21^\circ$ against $5.82^\circ$ for holding the last frame, $28\%$ lower. Constant-velocity
extrapolation is far worse than either ($13.56^\circ$), because it overshoots over a
one-second horizon. Both masks rank the cells as the loss axis does elsewhere:
reconstructing \textsf{p} alone is worst, and \textsf{pv} or \textsf{pk} is best. Adding
torque to the loss costs pose fidelity ($3.30^\circ$ against $3.04^\circ$ on average):
under a fixed decoder budget, a representation that also encodes dynamics keeps less
precise angles.

Before running this test we expected the inverse. A near-identity encoder reconstructs well
while abstracting nothing, so reconstruction error might rank cells opposite to their
downstream scores. The data do not show this. Lower reconstruction error goes with
\emph{higher} BABEL macro mAP over the sixteen cells (Spearman $-0.60$, $p=0.014$), and
lower forecasting error even more so ($-0.68$, $p=0.004$). In this matrix, the backbones
that keep the most signal in their tokens are also the ones a linear probe can read best.

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:exp-summary}

Three results hold across tests. First, the loss axis carries the channel matrix and the
input axis barely matters. Reconstructing velocity is the single step that moves every
benchmark: all three BABEL tasks, retrieval, reconstruction and forecasting. Second, the
advantage of pretraining over summary statistics grows with the temporal resolution the
task demands: $13\%$ on window classification, $21\%$ on single-action classification, $62\%$ on
temporal segmentation. Yet
boundary localization stays close to a constant predictor in every backbone. Third, the
torque target changes the representation substantially, widening it by nearly half, in
directions the action benchmarks do not read. On clinical severity no axis and no objective
can be ranked above seed noise.
