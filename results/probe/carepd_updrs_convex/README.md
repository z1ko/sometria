# CARE-PD UPDRS-gait convex probe

Frozen MAE backbone, mean pooling, one `Linear` head fit to convergence with L-BFGS
(`sometria.downstream.classifier_convex.MotionConvexClassifier`). The head has one global
optimum, so nothing here depends on a learning rate, warmup or epoch budget; the only
thing selected is `weight_decay`, and it is selected **once** on `carepd_BMCLab_6fold_1`
and reused everywhere, which is the paper's procedure (App. C).

Scored against CARE-PD's own published protocols (Adeli et al., NeurIPS 2025, S4.2), each
materialized as a named split set by `sometria.carepd.import_carepd_folds`, so the split
set *is* the protocol:

| protocol | train / eval | split sets |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| within-site LOSO | one cohort, one patient held out | 110 |
| cross-dataset | train on cohort x, test on cohort y | 12 |
| LODO | train on the other three, test on x | 4 |

MIDA is deliberately not run: it is another 110 folds answering "does a little in-domain
data recover the LODO loss", which only becomes interesting once LODO itself is settled.

Every number below is **pooled** -- each fold's held-out predictions scored once as one
set, not the mean of per-fold scores. This matters more than it sounds; see *Why pooled*
below. A take's prediction is the majority vote of its windows, per the paper (S4.1).

Produced by `goodnight_carepd.sh`, read from
`runs/probe/carepd_updrs_convex/protocols/`. Regenerate the tables and `metrics.csv`:

```
uv run python results/probe/carepd_updrs_convex/gen.py
```

## Reading

Unless a paragraph says otherwise, every number quoted below is
**`medium_100ep` / `in_pk__loss_pk`**, three seeds. That was the only architecture and cell
on disk when this was written; the generated tables above now carry two architectures and
nine cells, so a bare figure in prose would be ambiguous. Cross-architecture comparison
lives in [`results/decoder_capacity/`](../../decoder_capacity/README.md), not here.

### What can be claimed

**The probe learns real gait severity signal.** Within-site LOSO clears the
majority-class floor on every cohort, by 1.7x on 3DGait to 4.2x on PD-GaM. This is the
cleanest claim available here because within-cohort LOSO has *no site shortcut* -- one
capture rig, so knowing which site a walk came from carries no label information. Results
on the pooled multi-cohort splits (`carepd_fixed`, `carepd_6fold_<k>`) cannot support
that claim and are not reported in this folder.

**On PD-GaM we beat the published average.** 0.668 against 0.620 ±0.056 over the paper's
seven purpose-built motion encoders, about +0.9σ. On 3DGait we land on their mean exactly
(0.271 vs 0.271). Cross-dataset overall, 0.260 against ~0.286, sits well inside their
±11pp spread. An MAE pretrained on AMASS joint torques is competitive with encoders built
for this, on the largest cohort.

**BMCLab and T-SDU-PD are where we fall short** -- -1.1σ and -2.6σ. T-SDU-PD is the real
deficit and the one worth chasing.

### Cohort size flips within-site against cross-site

The finding that is not in the paper in this form. Ordering cohorts by size:

| cohort | takes | LOSO | LODO | winner |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | :--- |
| PD-GaM | 1700 | 0.668 | 0.372 | within, by 0.30 |
| BMCLab | 781 | 0.406 | 0.296 | within, by 0.11 |
| T-SDU-PD | 381 | 0.284 | **0.344** | **cross** |
| 3DGait | 90 | 0.271 | **0.278** | **cross** |

For the two small cohorts, training on the *other three sites* beats training on their own
patients, and the crossover sits between 381 and 781 takes. It is monotone in cohort size,
which makes it a usable rule of thumb for a new clinical site: below a few hundred walks,
borrow other sites rather than fitting your own.

### PD-GaM is the worst source despite being the largest

Cross-dataset by source: T-SDU-PD 0.292, 3DGait 0.281, BMCLab 0.279, **PD-GaM 0.188**.
Seed sd is ~0.02, so the gap is far outside noise. This inverts the paper, where PD-GaM
generalizes best "likely due to its scale and diversity".

The obvious suspect is that PD-GaM was captured at **25 Hz** and our pipeline resamples
everything to 60, making it by far the most interpolated corpus we hold -- and its
within-site score (0.668, our best) would be unaffected, because train and test are
interpolated identically. That is a hypothesis, not a result. It predicts the effect
should shrink if PD-GaM were held at 25 Hz, which nothing here tests.

### Why pooled, not the per-fold mean

A LOSO fold holds out one patient, who often walks at a single severity. Measured on
T-SDU-PD, 14 folds, same predictions scored two ways:

| metric | pooled | per-fold mean |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| macro mAP | 0.385 | **0.756** ±0.227 |
| macro F1 (0-3) | **0.256** | 0.157 ±0.095 |

Both directions are artefacts. mAP inflates because the only class present is positive
everywhere, so AP is 1.0 by construction -- it read exactly 1.0 on 5 of the 14 folds.
Macro F1 deflates because it divides across classes that patient never walks at. The
check that this is the explanation: BMCLab's 6-fold splits hold out 4 subjects instead of
1, and their pooled/per-fold gap collapses to 0.036.

The per-fold mean and sd are still in `metrics.csv`. The *spread* is genuine
between-subject variability; only the level is an artefact.

### Metrics

`macro_f1_0123` is the paper's and the only comparable number. `macro_f1_012` drops score
3 from the **average only** -- samples carrying it stay in, so a true-3 take predicted as
2 still counts against class 2 (S4.3). The `0-2 ÷ 0-3` column is exactly **1.333** for
BMCLab and T-SDU-PD because neither cohort contains a single score-3 take, so class 3
scores F1 0 and dropping it multiplies by 4/3. PD-GaM's 1.017 says the opposite: its 35
score-3 takes are actually being detected.

`macro_map` is kept because macro F1 depends on where argmax happens to fall and a
ranking metric does not. It is *not* used to select `weight_decay` here, for the reason
above.

## Caveats

- **One corpus, one architecture, one epoch budget.** Nothing here says whether the
  pretraining corpus matters for CARE-PD. `amass_motionx_clean` has not been run.
- **The two cells are indistinguishable** (paired t = +0.03 over 60 pairs), so `loss_pkd`
  and `loss_pk` are used interchangeably in the prose above. Do not read a cell ranking
  into these tables.
- **60 Hz against the paper's 30.** An unknown constant offset separates every
  paper comparison in this folder. Deliberate -- our preprocessing stays at 60.
- **`weight_decay` was tuned on `carepd_BMCLab_6fold_1`**, so BMCLab-target rows saw
  BMCLab data during tuning. True of the paper's numbers too, but the cross-site claim
  rests on the other three targets.
- **LODO has no error bar across folds**, only across seeds -- it is one split set per
  target by construction.
- **The paper's LODO column is a range read off a heatmap** (Fig. 4a), not a published
  table. Treat it as indicative.

## Gaps

- MIDA (110 folds) -- would say how much in-domain data recovers the LODO loss.
- `amass_motionx_clean` and the other seven matrix cells.
- The 25 Hz hypothesis for PD-GaM's poor transfer: re-import that cohort without
  upsampling and re-run cross-dataset.
- T-SDU-PD's -2.6σ within-site deficit is unexplained.
- QWK. UPDRS gait is ordinal and every metric here is ordinal-blind: confusing 0 with 3
  scores the same as 0 with 1.
