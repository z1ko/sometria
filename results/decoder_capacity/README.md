# Decoder capacity, and why the torque loss channel looks inert

**Status: suggestive, not established.** The direction is consistent across all three
input rows, but the pooled interaction is t = 1.71 over nine paired rows. An earlier
single-seed version of this page reported 9.5 sigma; that was a favourable seed draw
combined with a borrowed sigma, and it did not survive replication. Read the
`Superseded claims` section before quoting anything from this folder elsewhere.

The puzzle. On `amass_clean/medium_100ep`, BABEL-60 macro mAP over three seeds:

| loss channels | macro mAP | step |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| `p` | 0.3508 | |
| `pk` | 0.3645 | +0.0137 |
| `pkd` | 0.3658 | +0.0013 |

Adding velocity and acceleration to the reconstruction target buys ten times what adding
torque buys. The same null appears on CARE-PD across all 126 protocol split sets (paired
t = +0.03 over 60 group-seed pairs).

The hypothesis. The decoder computes torque itself, so the encoder never has to. `medium`
runs a decoder at 8.6x the encoder's FLOPs -- it reconstructs all 1290 tokens where the
encoder, at `mask_ratio` 0.90, sees 128. MAE's own default is 0.35x. Weaken the decoder
and the encoder should have to carry what the decoder can no longer compute.

## Reading

### The result

Weakening the decoder from 8.6x to 1.0x widens the pk-to-pkd gap by **+0.0046** on
average, pooled over all three input rows and three seeds. All three rows move in the
predicted direction. But **t = 1.71**, so at three seeds this does not clear even an
uncorrected two-sided 0.05 threshold. The strongest single row is `in_pk` at t = 2.58.

`in_pkd` moves least (+0.0010, t = 0.17), which is the predicted ordering: when torque is
already an encoder *input*, the decoder's ability to recompute it should matter least.
With nothing significant, that is a consistent sign rather than evidence.

**The weaker decoder is a worse model**, and that part is not marginal: -0.0049 paired
over 27 cell-seed pairs, t = -4.19, winning 10/27. It is also noisier -- seed sigma 0.0044
against 0.0025. So `medium_thindec` is not a free swap, and `medium` stays the default.

### What was ruled out

**Not "the two encoders are identical".** Linear CKA between their pooled features is
0.934, against 0.959 for two seeds of the *same* cell -- the torque target moves the
representation about 1.6x further than a seed does, and the two sets do not overlap. The
difference is real; the frozen mean-pool linear probe barely reads it.

**Not weight-space similarity.** Relative weight distance is 1.29 for the loss-term pair
and 1.34 for the seed pair, both near the sqrt(2) two *unrelated* weight vectors give.
Transformers have permutation and rotation symmetries, so weights can be maximally far
apart while computing nearly the same function. That is why the measurement above is CKA.

**Not "the torque term does nothing".** At `medium`/`in_pk` the gap is +0.0013 with sd
0.0002 across seeds -- **t = 10.14**. It is a real and extremely reproducible effect that
happens to be negligible in size. Tiny and absent are different things, and an unpaired
test calls this one 0.8 sigma.

### Why the mechanism is still plausible

Torque is not new information. Inverse dynamics is
`tau = M(q)qdd + C(q,qd)qd + G(q) - J^T F_ext`, so given the joint angles, velocities and
accelerations the encoder already receives, torque is determined except for external
forces -- measured at R^2 = 0.68 from a *linear* map, a lower bound because linear
regression cannot represent the bilinear `M(q)qdd` term.

A loss term only reshapes the encoder if it demands information the encoder would
otherwise drop. Torque mostly does not. Contrast `p` -> `pk`, which survives at any
decoder size: velocity and acceleration are temporal structure, and no decoder conjures
them from joint angles alone.

That story is consistent with everything measured here. It is just not *established* by
it, because the effect it predicts is the same size as the noise at three seeds.

## Superseded claims

Recorded because they were acted on, not to be tidy.

- **"`medium_thindec` + `loss_pkd` is free, 0.3647 against medium's 0.3658."** From one
  seed. The three-seed mean is 0.3604, and paired peak-to-peak the weaker decoder is
  -0.0035 (t = -4.07). It is not free.
- **"The gap widens 12x, 9.5 sigma."** From the same single seed, tested against sigma
  borrowed from `medium` (0.0011) when `thindec`'s own is 0.0044. The three-seed gap at
  `in_pk` is +0.0070, not +0.0109, and the interaction is t = 1.71.
- **"+0.0013 at medium is noise (1.2 sigma)."** Unpaired. Paired it is t = 10.14.

## Caveats

- **Three seeds.** Every t here has two degrees of freedom per row. The pooled interaction
  row is the only one worth much, and it is 1.71.
- **`medium_tinydec` is one seed and two cells.** Its -0.0149 level cost and +0.0159 gap
  are both single draws; it is in the tables for shape, not for inference.
- **One benchmark, one corpus, one epoch budget.** BABEL-60, `amass_clean`, 100 epochs.
- **`num_heads` is shared** between encoder and decoder, so `medium_tinydec` runs 8 heads
  over 64 dimensions, head_dim 8. If tinydec ever looks anomalous rather than just weaker,
  suspect that first.
- **The FLOP model is analytic**, not profiled: projections, attention scores and the
  feed-forward, ignoring norms, biases and kernel efficiency. Good enough for a ratio.

## Gaps

- **More seeds.** To resolve +0.0046 against sigma 0.0081 at 0.05 two-sided needs roughly
  n = 26 paired rows against the current 9 -- about six more seeds, or ~9h of pretraining.
  That is the cheapest way to settle this.
- **`medium_tinydec` at the full matrix and three seeds.** If the effect is real it should
  be largest there, and it is currently the weakest-evidenced rung.
- **The complementary test.** Does a *richer readout* recover the difference at 8.6x?
  Attentive pooling or a fine-tune would separate "the decoder absorbed it" from "the
  mean-pool linear probe cannot see it" -- the two explanations this page cannot yet tell
  apart.
- An intermediate rung: `dec_depth 2, dec_dim 128` is ~2.1x and untested.
